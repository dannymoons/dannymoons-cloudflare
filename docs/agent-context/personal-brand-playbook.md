## 1. Executive Summary

DannyMoons.nl should be the personal home base for Danny Moons: the person connecting sustainable software engineering, AI agents, modern web architecture, digital sustainability, and practical product building.

It should not become "just another developer blog." It should become a living notebook for a specific body of work:

> Building software that is faster, smarter, and more sustainable.

The site should make the following idea clear within seconds:

Danny is a software engineer and builder exploring how the web can become more sustainable, useful, intelligent, and human. Moonsio is the company. Orbit is a product. Hermes is an internal agentic workflow project. Pixel to Planet is an initiative. DannyMoons.nl is the personal narrative layer connecting all of those efforts into one visible, trusted, evolving body of thought.

### Strategic Role of DannyMoons.nl

DannyMoons.nl should do five jobs:

1. Establish Danny's point of view.
2. Document what Danny is learning while building.
3. Create a discoverable body of writing around sustainable software, AI, and modern web architecture.
4. Support Moonsio, Orbit, Pixel to Planet, Hermes, and open-source work without becoming a sales site for any single one.
5. Give Lotte a clear editorial system for producing high-quality drafts that sound like Danny, serve real readers, and build long-term authority.

### Core Positioning

**Short version:**  
Software engineer exploring sustainable software, AI, and the future of the web.

**Long version:**  
Danny Moons builds and writes about sustainable software engineering, AI agents, performance, modern web architecture, and digital products. Through Moonsio, Orbit, Pixel to Planet, and open-source experiments, he shares practical lessons from building systems that are faster, smarter, and more responsible.

### Strategic Bet

The next decade of software will be shaped by three overlapping forces:

- Software must become more sustainable.
- AI will change how software is designed, built, operated, and discovered.
- Developers and companies will need clearer judgment, not just more tools.

DannyMoons.nl should sit exactly at that intersection.

### The Site in One Sentence

DannyMoons.nl is a personal knowledge base for sustainable software engineering, AI-assisted development, and the future of digital products.

### Recommended Publishing Cadence

For the first year:

- Publish one substantial article every two weeks.
- Publish one shorter note, field observation, or project log in alternate weeks.
- Publish one monthly newsletter issue.
- Refresh one cornerstone article each month.
- Turn every substantial article into two to four social posts.

This creates a realistic rhythm of roughly:

- 24 long-form articles per year.
- 24 short posts or notes per year.
- 12 newsletters per year.
- 75 to 150 social posts per year, derived from deeper work.

### Content Strategy in One Line

Write what Danny is actually building, testing, noticing, and changing his mind about.

This matters. The web is already full of shallow summaries about AI, sustainability, and software. DannyMoons.nl should win by being more specific, more grounded, and more personal.

## 2. Brand Manifesto

### The Manifesto

Software is no longer weightless.

Every website, build process, cloud function, dependency, animation, AI request, analytics script, and deployment has a footprint. Some of that footprint is visible in performance budgets and hosting bills. Some of it is hidden in energy use, hardware cycles, operational complexity, human attention, and maintenance burden.

For years, the web rewarded more. More pages. More scripts. More tracking. More layers. More frameworks. More automation. More dashboards. More dashboards about dashboards.

But better software is not always more software.

The future belongs to people who can build useful systems with restraint. Systems that load quickly. Systems that respect users. Systems that are clear enough to maintain. Systems that use AI where it genuinely helps and avoid it where it only creates noise. Systems that treat performance, accessibility, privacy, and sustainability as part of quality, not as side quests after launch.

DannyMoons.nl exists to explore that future in public.

This is not a place for hype cycles. It is not a place for recycled thought leadership. It is not a place for pretending that every new tool is a revolution.

It is a working notebook for questions like:

- How do we make software more sustainable without making it joyless?
- How do AI agents change the way developers think, write, test, and ship?
- How do modern web architectures become simpler instead of heavier?
- How can companies measure the digital impact of their products honestly?
- How can builders stay useful when tools become more powerful every month?
- How do we keep craft, taste, and responsibility alive inside automated workflows?

The point is not to have all the answers.

The point is to build, measure, learn, explain, and improve.

DannyMoons.nl is the personal layer behind that work. It connects company work, product work, experiments, open source, sustainability research, and practical engineering lessons into one clear body of thought.

The promise is simple:

> Build software with more intelligence, less waste, and better judgment.

### Beliefs

1. **Sustainability is an engineering quality.**  
   If software is slow, wasteful, bloated, or hard to maintain, it is not truly high quality.

2. **Performance is user respect.**  
   Fast systems reduce frustration, expand access, lower infrastructure costs, and often reduce environmental impact.

3. **AI should increase judgment, not replace it.**  
   AI can help people explore, draft, refactor, test, and operate systems. But responsibility stays with humans.

4. **The best software is often smaller than expected.**  
   Complexity is expensive. Useful simplicity is an advanced skill.

5. **Documentation is part of building.**  
   If a system cannot be explained, it probably cannot be maintained well.

6. **The future of the web needs builders, not spectators.**  
   Real authority comes from making things, shipping things, breaking things, and sharing the lessons.

7. **Sustainable software is not only carbon.**  
   It includes energy, infrastructure, maintainability, accessibility, privacy, attention, cost, and lifespan.

8. **Personal brand is a trust system.**  
   A strong personal site helps people understand what Danny believes, how he works, and why his projects exist.

### Enemies

The brand is not built against people. It is built against habits:

- Performance theater.
- Greenwashing.
- AI hype without practice.
- Content written only to rank.
- Bloated technical stacks.
- Shallow benchmark claims.
- Architecture without maintenance thinking.
- Sustainability treated as a marketing checkbox.
- Automation without accountability.
- Endless tool adoption without taste.

### Desired Reputation

In three years, people should say:

- "Danny writes clearly about sustainable software without making it vague."
- "He connects AI, web performance, and sustainability in a practical way."
- "He actually builds the things he talks about."
- "His articles are useful when I need to make engineering decisions."
- "He has a thoughtful point of view, not just opinions about tools."

### Brand Promise

DannyMoons.nl helps developers, founders, and digital teams build software that is more sustainable, intelligent, and maintainable.

### Brand Tension

The central creative tension:

> The web needs innovation, but it also needs restraint.

This tension should appear throughout the writing. Danny is not anti-technology. He is not anti-AI. He is not anti-framework. He is pro-judgment.

### Messaging House

| Product building | Ideas become real through shipping. | Orbit, Carbonfooter, Moonsio, Pixel to Planet, open-source tools. |

### Primary Messaging Pillars

#### 1. Better Software Uses Less Waste

Waste appears in many forms:

- Unused JavaScript.
- Overbuilt infrastructure.
- Heavy media.
- Tracking scripts nobody reads.
- Slow pages.
- Duplicate tooling.
- Low-quality AI output.
- Poor documentation.
- Rework caused by unclear decisions.

Danny's writing should help readers identify and remove waste without becoming dogmatic.

#### 2. AI Needs Taste and Responsibility

The question is not "Can AI do this?"

The better question is:

> Should AI do this, and what kind of human review makes the result trustworthy?

AI should be discussed as a workflow material. It can be powerful, but the brand should always emphasize responsibility, review, transparency, and usefulness.

#### 3. Sustainability Is a Product Quality

Sustainability is not only an ethical concern. It can improve:

- Speed.
- Conversion.
- Reliability.
- Maintainability.
- Cost.
- Accessibility.
- Trust.

This framing makes sustainability valuable for developers, founders, marketers, and decision-makers.

#### 4. The Web Still Matters

AI changes discovery and interface patterns, but the open web remains a critical layer for trust, sources, ownership, and distribution. DannyMoons.nl should defend the value of a well-maintained personal website.

#### 5. Build in Public, Think in Systems

The personal brand should show the work. Project notes, architecture decisions, changelogs, failures, and experiments make Danny more credible than polished claims alone.

| Level | Message |
|---|---|
| Functional | Learn practical approaches to web performance, sustainability, AI workflows, and modern architecture. |

## 6. Brand Voice

### Voice Attributes

DannyMoons.nl should sound:

- Clear.
- Practical.
- Curious.
- Warm.
- Opinionated but fair.
- Technical without being needlessly dense.
- Honest about uncertainty.
- Builder-led.
- Calm around hype.

### Voice Formula

> Practical engineering clarity + personal curiosity + sustainable systems thinking.

### Writing Principles

1. **Start from a real problem.**  
   Avoid abstract intros that could belong on any site.

2. **Use specific examples.**  
   Mention concrete tools, decisions, measurements, mistakes, and trade-offs.

3. **Write from experience.**  
   If Danny has not personally tested something, say so.

4. **Separate fact, opinion, and hypothesis.**  
   The reader should know when Danny knows something, believes something, or is testing something.

5. **Prefer clarity over cleverness.**  
   A memorable sentence is good. A useful sentence is better.

6. **Respect the reader's intelligence.**  
   Do not over-explain basic concepts unless the article is explicitly introductory.

7. **Do not posture.**  
   No fake certainty. No dramatic hype. No "everything has changed forever" unless the article proves it.

8. **Keep sustainability practical.**  
   Avoid guilt as the main emotional lever. Focus on agency, quality, and better decisions.

### The Danny Voice

Danny should sound like a builder who thinks deeply while working. The voice can be personal, but it should not become diary-like unless the article is clearly a note or retrospective.

Good Danny-style sentences:

- "I do not think sustainable software starts with a carbon calculator. It starts with asking why this code, page, or system needs to exist."
- "AI helped me move faster here, but only after I changed the review process around it."
- "The performance issue was not the framework. It was the number of small decisions we had stopped noticing."
- "This is where sustainability becomes engineering work instead of branding."

Weak sentences:

- "In today's fast-paced digital world, sustainability is more important than ever."
- "AI is revolutionizing everything."
- "This ultimate guide will teach you all the best practices."
- "Green websites are the future."

### Tone by Content Type

| Content Type | Tone |
|---|---|
| Manifesto | Personal, sharp, values-led |
| Technical tutorial | Direct, precise, example-heavy |
| Case study | Honest, structured, evidence-led |
| Opinion essay | Clear stance, fair counterarguments |
| Project update | Conversational, reflective, useful |
| Newsletter | Friendly, compact, curated |
| Speaking page | Confident, simple, professional |
| About page | Human, grounded, story-led |

### Editorial Point of View

Danny's writing should often include:

- The decision behind the decision.
- What was measured.
- What changed after testing.
- What trade-off was accepted.
- What surprised him.
- What he would do differently next time.

### Voice Rules for Lotte

Lotte may draft, structure, research, summarize, and propose angles. But Lotte should never invent Danny's experience.

If a draft needs a personal detail, Lotte should mark it as:

`[Danny: add personal example here]`

If a draft includes a claim that needs verification, Lotte should mark it as:

`[Verify before publishing]`

If a draft includes an opinion, Lotte should make it sound like a considered perspective, not universal truth.

## 7. Visual Identity Direction

### Visual Strategy

The visual identity should feel like a thoughtful technical notebook, not a corporate SaaS site and not a generic personal portfolio.

It should be:

- Quiet.
- Precise.
- Warm.
- Technical.
- Sustainable without looking like a stereotypical green brand.
- Slightly experimental.
- Easy to read.

### Design Keywords

- Notebook.
- Systems.
- Orbit.
- Measurement.
- Craft.
- Signal.
- Flow.
- Grounded.
- Future-facing.
- Calm interface.

### Avoid

- Overused green eco visuals.
- Leaf icons as the main sustainability symbol.
- Dark-blue developer SaaS sameness.
- Purple AI gradients.
- Beige lifestyle minimalism.
- Stock photos of forests with laptops.
- Decorative complexity.
- Huge marketing cards everywhere.
- Dense dashboard styling on editorial pages.

### Color Direction

Use a restrained but not one-note palette.

Recommended palette direction:

| Role | Color Idea | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Background | Soft off-white or very light gray | Main reading surface |
| Text | Near-black with slight warmth | Primary text |
| Muted text | Cool gray | Metadata, captions |
| Accent 1 | Deep green-blue | Sustainability and links |
| Accent 2 | Warm amber | Highlights, active states |
| Accent 3 | Muted violet or blue | AI and systems references |
| Code surface | Soft charcoal or light code block | Technical examples |

Important: do not let the entire site become green. Sustainability should be communicated through clarity, performance, and restraint, not just color.

### Typography

Recommended direction:

- Strong, readable sans-serif for UI and headings.
- Comfortable serif or highly readable sans-serif for long-form articles.
- Monospace for code, metadata, and small technical labels.

Typography should make long articles feel inviting. DannyMoons.nl will rely heavily on written content, so reading quality is brand quality.

### Layout Principles

- Use generous article margins.
- Keep line length comfortable, around 65 to 80 characters for body copy.
- Use strong section hierarchy.
- Keep navigation simple.
- Give project pages enough visual identity without turning them into mini landing pages.
- Let code, diagrams, and tables breathe.
- Use "notes" and "field logs" as lighter formats so not every piece feels like a major essay.

### Imagery Direction

Primary imagery should come from:

- Screenshots of real projects.
- Diagrams.
- Interface details.

## 10. SEO and AI-Search Strategy

### Strategic Principle

SEO for DannyMoons.nl should be a distribution system for useful work, not a reason to create generic content.

Google's current official guidance continues to emphasize helpful, reliable, people-first content and E-E-A-T: experience, expertise, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness. Google's guidance on generative AI features also says SEO fundamentals still matter because AI search features rely on the search index and ranking systems. Bing similarly emphasizes technical quality, clarity, accessibility, original value, and avoidance of manipulative "GEO" tactics.

This means the strategy should be:

> Make the site crawlable, structured, fast, trustworthy, useful, and clearly associated with Danny's areas of expertise.

Not:

> Publish hundreds of shallow pages for every possible AI-search query variation.

## 14. Writing Guidelines for Lotte

### Lotte's Role

Lotte is Danny's AI assistant for research, structure, drafting, editing, and repurposing. Lotte helps Danny think and publish more consistently. Lotte does not replace Danny's judgment, experience, or accountability.

### Lotte's Responsibilities

Lotte can:

- Propose article angles.
- Create outlines.
- Draft sections.
- Turn rough notes into polished drafts.
- Suggest titles and metadata.
- Identify missing proof.
- Create social post variants.
- Build newsletter drafts.
- Summarize sources.
- Create checklists and templates.
- Flag weak claims.
- Suggest internal links.

Lotte must not:

- Invent Danny's personal experiences.
- Invent measurements.
- Invent client results.
- Invent quotes.
- Present uncertain claims as facts.
- Publish without Danny's review.
- Add search keywords unnaturally.
- Create content only to manipulate rankings.
- Use AI as the listed author.

### Lotte's Drafting Workflow

1. **Clarify intent**  
   Identify audience, article type, primary thesis, and reader outcome.

2. **Gather inputs**  
   Use Danny's notes, project logs, measurements, screenshots, code, or external sources.

3. **Create outline**  
   Include thesis, sections, examples needed, and open questions.

4. **Draft**  
   Write in Danny's voice, with markers for missing personal detail.

5. **Self-review**  
   Check for generic claims, weak structure, missing examples, and unsourced facts.

6. **Danny review**  
   Danny adds lived experience, corrects judgment, and approves claims.

7. **SEO and AI-search pass**  
   Add metadata, internal links, summary, structured headings, and related content.

8. **Publish**  
   Publish only after final human review.

9. **Repurpose**  
   Create newsletter and social snippets.

10. **Measure**  
   Track performance and feed learnings back into the editorial calendar.

### Lotte's Article Brief Template

```markdown
# Article Brief

## Working title

## Article type
Essay / guide / tutorial / case study / project note / opinion / checklist

## Primary audience

## Reader problem

## Thesis

## What the reader should be able to do or understand after reading

## Danny's personal inputs needed
- [Danny: add example]
- [Danny: add measurement]
- [Danny: add project note]

## Sources needed

## Related DannyMoons.nl content

## Related project

## Draft outline

```

### Lotte's Draft Quality Checklist

Before showing Danny a draft, Lotte should check:

- Does the article have a clear thesis?
- Is the opening specific?
- Is there at least one concrete example?
- Are claims marked for verification?
- Are Danny-specific experiences marked instead of invented?
- Is the article useful without needing to rank?
- Are sections scannable?
- Is the tone calm and practical?
- Are sustainability claims careful?
- Are AI claims not overhyped?
- Are internal links suggested?
- Is the conclusion useful rather than generic?

### Lotte's Research Rules

When researching:

- Prefer primary sources.
- Capture source links.
- Note publication dates for fast-changing topics.
- Separate source facts from interpretation.
- Do not overquote.
- Summarize in Danny's language.
- Flag conflicts between sources.
- Flag where guidance may change.

### Lotte's Style Rules

Use:

- Short paragraphs.
- Clear headings.
- Concrete nouns.
- Active voice.
- Practical examples.
- Honest caveats.
- Tables for comparisons.
- Checklists for workflows.

Avoid:

- "In today's rapidly evolving digital landscape."
- "It is important to note."
- "Game-changing."
- "Revolutionary."
- "Unlock."
- "Leverage" unless it truly fits.
- "This comprehensive guide."
- Fake urgency.
- Generic conclusions.

### Lotte's Human Detail Markers

Use these markers:

- `[Danny: add personal example]`
- `[Danny: confirm this claim]`
- `[Danny: add project screenshot]`
- `[Danny: add measurement]`
- `[Danny: decide whether to mention client/project]`
- `[Verify source before publishing]`
- `[Potential legal/compliance review]`

### AI Disclosure Rule

If Lotte materially helped create a piece, add a production note when reasonable:

> Production note: This article was drafted or edited with assistance from Lotte, Danny's AI assistant, and reviewed by Danny before publication.

If the piece covers public-interest topics, regulation, or synthetic media, use stronger review and disclosure standards.
