## 6. Core Personal Stance on Sustainable Web

This section is the full stance. It is also mirrored in the Obsidian vault at `/home/danny/vault/wiki/concepts/danny-personal-sustainable-web-development-stance.md` but the content below is the canonical version for agent use.

### Core belief

Sustainable web development is about reducing the environmental impact of websites and digital services. That impact is shaped by many decisions across the full lifecycle of a project: strategy, content, design, development, hosting, third-party services, optimisation, maintenance, and measurement.

### Main motivation

Reducing waste, bloat, and overengineering. This includes:

- unnecessary features
- unnecessary code
- unnecessary dependencies
- oversized media
- excessive JavaScript
- avoidable third-party scripts
- overcomplicated architectures
- marketing technology that does not clearly earn its place
- design or content choices that create avoidable digital weight

The goal is not minimalism for its own sake, but intentionality: build what is needed, build it well, and avoid avoidable waste.

### Responsibility

Sustainable web development is a shared responsibility across the whole chain: developers, designers, agencies, clients, hosting providers, platform companies, content teams, tool builders, and users. No single group can solve it alone, but design and development carry a lot of practical responsibility because many sustainability outcomes are shaped before editors or users interact with the site.

### Carbon claims and measurement

Carbon numbers can be useful, but they need to be communicated carefully. It is acceptable to use carbon estimates when the method, assumptions, and limitations are explained. The numbers should not be presented as perfect or absolute truth. Measurement is useful for awareness, directional diagnostics, client reporting, comparison, improvement tracking, and making invisible impact easier to discuss. Transparency matters: a tool should explain what it measures, what it does not measure, and how conclusions are reached.

### Green hosting

Green hosting is foundational, but only one part of the picture. A site can run on green hosting and still be bloated, slow, or poorly designed. Green hosting should be combined with efficient architecture, good caching, optimised media, reduced page weight, fewer unnecessary requests, responsible content and design decisions, and careful use of third-party scripts.

### Performance and sustainability

Sustainable web development is broader than performance optimisation. Performance is an important lever, but sustainability also includes hosting choices, architecture, content strategy, design decisions, third-party scripts, accessibility, maintainability, lifecycle thinking, privacy and ethics, client education, and responsible measurement. A fast website is not automatically sustainable, but many sustainable choices also improve performance.

### Design and creativity

Sustainable web development is not anti-design or anti-creativity. Rich design is allowed, but heavier choices should be justified and optimised aggressively. The question should be: "Does this design choice create enough value to justify its cost?" If the answer is yes, it should still be implemented as efficiently as possible.

### Greenwashing risks

- vague claims like "eco-friendly website" without evidence
- overprecise carbon numbers without explaining uncertainty
- presenting green hosting as proof that the whole website is sustainable
- making claims without methodology
- reducing sustainability to one score
- ignoring trade-offs and limitations

Credible sustainability communication should be specific, transparent, evidence-based, and honest about caveats.

### Client communication

Sustainable web development should be explained to clients as both a responsibility story and a better-website story. Important angles include: climate impact, better performance, more conscious choices, less waste, better maintainability, improved user experience, responsible brand behaviour, and lower complexity over time. The message should not only be "this emits less CO₂" but also "this creates a better, leaner, more maintainable, more responsible website."

### Client compromise approach

When a client wants something that feels unsustainable but commercially understandable:
1. Talk it through and try to convince them with evidence, reasoning, and trade-offs.
2. Offer lighter or better alternatives that still meet the business goal.
3. If the client insists, accept the choice where appropriate, but clearly state the caveats and the sustainability stance.

This approach is pragmatic but principled: challenge unsustainable choices, propose better paths, and compromise transparently when needed.

### Red lines and caveats

Dark patterns, invasive tracking, excessive marketing technology, unnecessary heavy media, unnecessary animation without clear value, closed platforms that block optimisation or hosting choice, claims that exaggerate sustainability impact, and technical choices that create avoidable long-term complexity are all sustainability concerns because they involve waste, ethics, control, maintainability, and avoidable impact.

### One-sentence stance

Sustainable web development means reducing the environmental impact of digital products by building less wastefully, choosing responsible infrastructure, optimising by default, questioning unnecessary complexity, and communicating impact transparently — while staying practical enough to work with real clients and business goals.

## 8. Claims Policy

Allowed claims:
- Danny builds websites with sustainable practices
- Danny founded Moonsio, Carbonfooter, Pixel To Planet
- Projects have achieved measurable improvements (with client approval for specifics)

Claims that need evidence or approval:
- Specific carbon or energy savings figures
- "Leading" or "expert" authority claims
- Client testimonials or outcome numbers without client approval

Claims to avoid:
- Guarantees of outcomes for future projects
- Negative claims about named individuals or companies
- Medical or health claims (those belong to Duizeligheid Nederland)
- Exaggerated sustainability impact
